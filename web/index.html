Products

Product names become the columns of workers.xlsx and the types referenced in orders.xlsx. Renaming / deleting a product updates worker abilities and orders automatically.

    Parts (VC)

    One VC (3-digit code) per row: VC code + difficulty (High/Low/None, used to match worker abilities) + repeat design hours + first-order design hours. An hours value of 0 counts as 0.03; if first-order hours are blank, repeat hours are always used. Check hours = design × check ratio (set below; default 0.3, can be calibrated from actual hours on the Hour Log tab in one click). Importing parts.xlsx reads by column: column 1 = VC code, column 8 = repeat design hours, column 9 = first-order design hours.

    Worker abilities

    Ability has two roles: Design and Check. Each product has two cells: the worker's design grade and check grade (A/B/C, blank = doesn't do that role). For a worker who only checks, leave design blank, and vice versa. A = High/Low/None, B = Low/None, C = None only. Grades only decide whether someone can do a difficulty; hours come from the VC table (check = design × check ratio, see the Parts tab). When you import orders.xlsx, the designers/checkers in the orders are added here automatically (fill in their abilities).

    Add products on the Products tab first before filling in the worker ability matrix.

    Leave records Only affects the Schedule: on leave days the entered hours are subtracted from team capacity (blank = full day, or any amount less than a shift, e.g. 3 or 5.5); does not affect Analysis or Retrospective.

    No leave records yet. When someone takes leave, click "+ Add leave", pick the worker and enter the start/end dates; leave "Leave per day (h)" blank for a full day, or enter half a day / a few hours.

    Orders

    One order per row: product (label) + VC list + designer + checker + due date (deadline) + created date (decides first order vs repeat) + actual finish date. "Import orders.xlsx" detects the header automatically (scans the whole sheet for the header row and matches columns by keywords like Sales Order/Item, VC, Designer/Checker, due/created/finish dates and product, skipping distractors such as "design hours"), then shows a mapping dialog for you to check/adjust before importing; if nothing is recognized it prefills the old template's column order (1 = VC · 3 = designer · 4 = checker · 5 = product · 6 = due · 7 = created · 8 = actual finish).

    #Order no.ProductVC listDesignerCheckerDue dateCreated date

    Analysis
    
      
    Adds up the theoretical hours of all orders (design = Σ(VC hours) × coefficient; check = design × check ratio, set on the Parts tab or calibrated from the hour log) and compares them with the actual logged hours imported on the Hour Log tab: ② everyone (theory vs actual totals, difference, ratio) + ③ per worker (theoretical hours from order assignments vs logged hours) + ④ order hours detail. For the comparison to hold, the orders and the hour log must cover the same period.
    
    
    
    
    
      
      
    
    
    
    
  
    

  
  
    
    
    
      
    Schedule (by due date)
    
      
    Only schedules unfinished orders (no "Actual finish date"), forward from today; completed ones are on the Retrospective tab. Three methods, each consuming total team capacity continuously and giving every order an expected start/finish date: ① Sequential — ignores due dates, queues by created date, finishes one then starts the next; ② By due date (slot) — earliest due first; when a batch finishes early, later tasks automatically fill the free time before their due dates; ③ Close to due date (reference) — starts as late as possible and finishes a few workdays before the due date (adjustable buffer). ① and ② have the same daily total load; they differ only in which order goes first. For ability bottlenecks see Analysis.

    Retrospective
    
      
    The retrospective only analyzes completed orders (with an "Actual finish date"), range = earliest created date → today; unfinished work is on the Schedule tab. It shows real delivery performance (on-time rate / lateness), the completion-speed distribution, and improvements without adding headcount (training existing workers). The more complete the data, the more accurate it is.
